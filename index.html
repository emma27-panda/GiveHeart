Health information that helps others

Share these basics with family, coworkers and neighbors.

Stroke: act FAST

  • Face drooping on one side
  • Arm weakness or numbness
  • Speech slurred or confused
  • Time to call 911 right away

Heart attack

  • Chest pressure, squeezing or pain
  • Pain in the arm, back, neck or jaw
  • Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea
  • Women may have milder chest pain and more fatigue or nausea
In an emergency, call 911. Do not drive yourself. Poison Control (US): 1-800-222-1222.

Wash your hands

Scrub with soap for at least 20 seconds: palms, backs, between fingers and under nails. Use hand sanitizer with 60% alcohol if there is no sink.

Get vaccinated

A yearly flu shot is recommended for most people 6 months and older. Ask a pharmacist or doctor which other vaccines you need.

Sleep and movement

Most adults need 7 or more hours of sleep. About 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, like brisk walking, supports heart and mood.

Know your numbers

Blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol often show no symptoms. Regular checkups catch problems early.

Check in on someone

  • Ask directly how they are doing
  • Listen without rushing to fix
  • Offer a specific help, like a meal or a ride
  • Follow up a few days later

Caring for caregivers

Burnout is common among healthcare workers. Rest, peer support and talking to a counselor are signs of strength, not weakness.

In crisis or thinking about suicide? Call or text 988 (US Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), any time. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.
